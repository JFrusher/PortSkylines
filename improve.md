Here is a scorched-earth restructuring of your remaining epics into a clean, highly actionable blueprint for Claude Code. This organizes your 19 gripes and vision points into four architectural pillars that transform *Port Skylines* from its current prototype state into a true, deep simulation twin.

---

## Pillar 1: Spatial & Structural Architecture (The Grid & Road Overhaul)

*Scorched-earth reset of rigid constraints to support true port topology.*

* **Ditch Square Tiles:** Replace fixed square grid limits with a flexible boundary/district-based expansion model (inspired by Cities: Skylines' tile-free/flexible area buying or modular district expansion), allowing organic coastline growth.
* **Advanced Road & Spline Network:** Rebuild the road system to support robust hierarchy (Arterials, Collectors, Dockside Local roads) with proper intersection handling, lane math, and scalable traffic junction simulation.
* **Quays as Functional Buildings & CS2 Addons:**
* Convert quays from static terrain props into functional building nodes.
* Implement **Cities: Skylines 2 style snap-on building addons** for quays—allowing players to snap road corridors, rail tracks, or utility lines directly behind Ship-to-Shore (STS) gantry cranes along the waterfront edge.



---

## Pillar 2: Maritime Logistics & Yard Operations ("Feeds & Speeds")

*Deepening the physical and temporal logistics loop.*

* **Contracts & Weekly Calendar:** Overhaul the contract system onto a strict **weekly scheduling calendar**. Contracts should feature specific delivery windows, volume quotas, penalty clauses, and high-stakes freight demands.
* **AGV & Yard Overhaul (Industries DLC Model):**
* Treat container yards like *Cities: Skylines* Industries DLC: paintable zones anchored by core buildings (AGV Depots, Yard Gatehouses, Maintenance Bays).
* Simulate AGVs (Automated Guided Vehicles) as true moving agents that queue at gates, pick up containers from stacking blocks, and interface directly with terminal bays.


* **Advanced Cargo Specialization (Difficulty Scaling):** Introduce complex cargo constraints that increase simulation difficulty:
* *Reefers:* Require continuous shore power/refrigeration tracking.
* *Sensitive/Hazardous:* Require specialized handling bays or safety buffers.
* *Rush/Priority Containers:* Strict time-to-dock deadlines affecting port rating.


* **Maritime Routes & Harbor Infrastructure:** Add dedicated deep-water **ship lanes**, tugboat depots for docking assistance (reducing berth turnaround times), and clear maritime traffic management.

---

## Pillar 3: Economy, Utilities & Infinite Scaling

*Balancing the macro simulation and late-game optimization loop.*

* **Economy Restructure (Cost-per-Container):** Rip out the traditional CS tax window and replace it with a port-specific economic model driven by **cost-per-container metrics**, operational overhead (crane maintenance, AGV fuel/power), and demurrage penalties.
* **Port Utilities & Infrastructure ("Things"):** Expand the utility layer beyond basic water/power to include data pipelines, charging stations for electric AGVs, heavy fuel depots, and grid substations.
* **Feeds, Speeds & Optimization:** Balance throughput bottlenecks (crane cycle times, gate throughput, stack density) to give players that classic, endlessly satisfying *Cities: Skylines* late-game optimization and enlargement itch.

## Pillar 4: Presentation & UI/UX Teardown

*A ground-up rebuild of player interaction and immersion.*

* **Massive UI Redesign:** Scrap the current UI completely. Build a proper Cities: Skylines-inspired UX hierarchy: clean toolbar layout, contextual info-views (traffic, logistics flow, container types, power/data grids), and readable financial dashboards.
* **Animations & Polish:** Implement synchronized, satisfying animations across all heavy machinery (STS crane hoists, spreaders grabbing containers, AGV wheel articulation, tugboats nudging cargo ships).
* **Game Life-Cycle Progression:** Design the macro progression loop across your remaining epics (Epics 5 through 11)—moving players from a small regional pier to a massive, automated mega-port.
