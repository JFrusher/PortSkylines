# Build the port Cities: Skylines never simulated

The released market has an empty slot that Port Skylines can fill. No released PC game lets you design a container terminal (berths, quay cranes, yard blocks, gate, on-dock rail) and then watch it run as a simulated flow. At least four indie port builders have been announced, though, and the most credible one, Port Control from the solo developer of Rail Route, is dated for 2027 ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2051160/Port_Control/)). The recommended roadmap has four parts. First, one short foundations phase to remove M0's structural shortcuts: a fixed-tick deterministic sim, versioned saves, chunked MultiMesh rendering and data-driven content. Second, deepen the terminal loop so it becomes a real planning game (berth windows, crane rates, yard density, dwell, gate queues). Third, get a renamed Steam page live as soon as there is a good-looking vertical slice. Fourth, enter Early Access with a narrow, stable, performant core rather than a wide, half-working one, which is the lesson of Cities: Skylines II. The evidence-based estimate for a solo developer is about 2 to 2.5 years from M0 to Early Access and then 2 to 4 years in Early Access. A team of 3 to 6 can roughly halve the pre-EA phase. The name is the most urgent problem. Paradox publicly claims SKYLINES on its own as a trademark, and the Scrolls precedent shows that a single shared word in the same goods class can be enough for a refusal ([Paradox: LEGO Skylines](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/lego-skylines/about); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/bethesda-mojang-settle-scrolls-trademark-lawsuit)). Rename before building any wishlists. This report is not legal advice.

## Four announced rivals are closing a gap nobody has shipped into

The released games split into two camps, and neither builds a terminal. The first camp is "drive the equipment" sims. **Docked** (Saber, PC/PS5/Xbox, March 2026, $29.99) models STS cranes, RTGs, straddle carriers and reach stackers with physics, but its layouts are fixed and upgrades are unlocked rather than designed. It sits at **73% positive across 1,301 Steam reviews**, has a critic average of 72, and reviewers' main complaint is repetition ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2487300/Docked/); [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/20196/docked); [The Xbox Hub](https://www.thexboxhub.com/docked-review/)) **[Fact]**. **Container Terminal Simulator** is rail-only, has no ships and had **13 reviews (Mixed)** after its 1.0 in September 2026 ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3806270/Container_Terminal_Simulator/)) **[Fact]**. The second camp is transport and city games where the port is a single node. In CS1 the cargo harbor is one building. In CS2's Bridges & Ports DLC ($19.99, **52% positive**) the port is a gate plus upgrade modules with no crane or yard logistics, and Steam negatives complain about the price ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2427743); [Paradox dev diary](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/cities-skylines-ii/news/bridges-and-ports-dev-diary-ports)) **[Fact]**. Transport Fever 2, Anno 1800 and OpenTTD treat harbours as stations or piers ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1066780/discussions/0/1740009710702649480)) **[Fact]**.

The announced pipeline is where the threat lies. **Portscape** (Inner Core Studios, "coming soon") is the closest concept. You "lay roads and railways, plan quays and container yards", "connect the quay, yard, gate and rail terminal", either operate equipment or "set automation policies", and handle transhipment. It is presented as a **"colourful miniature world" with Cozy and Casual tags** ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5334580/Portscape/)) **[Fact]**. **Port Control** (2027) is a dispatcher and puzzle game with a "night control-room look". It covers berths, pilots, tugs and tides, with an automatic counterpart for every manual job. Its developer has already shipped Rail Route on Steam and Switch ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2051160/Port_Control/); [GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/contents/49979-rail-route-boards-the-switch-today)) **[Fact]**. **Harbor Master** (isometric, contracts, research for larger ships) and **Seaport Tycoon** (page up since about 2021 with no date, possibly stalled) complete the field ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5169090); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1580430)) **[Fact]**. There is no wishlist or follower data for any of them, because SteamDB was unreachable **[Gap]**.

**[Inference]** The open position is "realistic planner at city-builder scale": a 3D, CS-style view in which the player designs quay length, crane count, yard block geometry, gate lanes and rail sidings, and the sim's honest throughput maths is the challenge. Portscape owns cozy and hands-on. Port Control owns dispatching. Docked owns driving. Port Skylines should not try to win on any of those three. Docked's sales are an encouraging signal that a container-terminal theme sells outside the core city-builder audience: about **39k to 78k PC units in about seven months** using a 30x to 60x review multiplier **[Estimate]** ([Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/boxleiter-method-explained)). Comparable logistics builders list at $25 to $40, and small port titles at $5 to $15. That points to **$19.99 to $24.99 at EA launch** **[Inference]** ([Captain of Industry](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1594320); [Rise of Industry](https://store.steampowered.com/app/671440); [Global Magnates](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2644590)). Adjacent games' complaints give firm design rules: provide time acceleration (the top complaint about Ships At Sea is 20 to 30 minutes of travel with no speed-up) ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1266540)), avoid a repetitive loop (Docked), ship stable builds (Container Terminal Simulator), and give real depth rather than "port equals one building" (CS2 DLC) **[Inference]**. A Steam search for "container port" returns almost nothing, so the keyword space is open ([Steam search API](https://store.steampowered.com/api/storesearch/?term=container%20port&cc=us&l=en)) **[Fact]**.

## Copy Cities: Skylines' traffic and milestones; avoid its launch

The genre is carried by the road network acting on simulated agents. Reviewers in 2015 praised CS1's traffic as "really really good" but said the city-running part lacked depth ([Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/2015-end-of-year-roundup/)). The studio came from Cities in Motion and brought that traffic-flow strategy with it ([Shamus Young](https://www.shamusyoung.com/twentysidedtale/?p=26174)) **[Fact]**. CS1's routing was proximity-based and ignored the actual network. CS2 moved to a path cost made of **time, comfort (turn avoidance, parking) and money (fuel, fees)**, with full lane use ([Colossal Order Dev Diary #2](https://colossalorder.fi/?p=1597); [Paradox Traffic AI](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/cities-skylines-ii/features/traffic-ai); [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/this-is-how-cities-skylines-2-vastly-improves-on-the-original-games-traffic-ai/)) **[Fact]**. Even so, players reported lane-hogging after launch ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/949230/discussions/0/3951406749575538437), anecdotal), and Bridges & Ports-era reviews still cite "significant traffic issues" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/cities-skylines-2/bridges-and-ports-out-now)). TM:PE (lane arrows, priority signs, timed lights, vehicle restrictions) is one of the most-subscribed CS1 mods, at about 3.5M subscribers by a third-party count ([Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/app/255710/workshop/Mods); [howtovideogame.com](https://howtovideogame.com/cities-skylines-best-mods/); [discovermods.com](https://discovermods.com/cities-skylines/top/); [CrazyKinux](https://www.crazykinux.ca/2023/03/the-top-and-best-15-cities-skylines-mods.html)) **[Fact]**. **[Inference]** For a port, lorries on gate roads are where the gameplay is. Weighted-cost routing, plus vanilla truck-only lanes, one-way gate lanes and simple signal timing, should ship in the base game rather than being left to mods.

The most consistent cargo complaint in CS1 is **truck spam**. Industries buildings generate "a massive amount of trucks", and players use warehouses as buffers and push freight onto rail and ships ([guidestrats](https://guidestrats.com/cities-skylines-cargo-hub-tips/); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/255710/discussions/0/3826416272372996988); [Paradox forum, Industries Dev Diary 7](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/cities-skylines-industries-dev-diary-7-cargo.1124492/post-24762897)) **[Fact]**. In CS2, reviewers liked the modular ports but noted that ships cannot route outside city limits and that cargo stays abstract ([MegaBearsFan](http://www.megabearsfan.net/post/2025/12/21/Cities-Skylines-2-Bridges-and-Ports-expansion-review.aspx)) **[Fact]**. **[Inference]** Port Skylines should make modal split (truck, rail, later barge) and gate buffering explicit levers, and show ship routes arriving from an off-map "outside world" on a visible schedule.

## Real terminal maths makes good game rules

Real terminal operations already form a tidy game system. Ship class sets draft and length. Draft gates the channel and berth depth, which requires dredging. Beam sets the crane outreach needed. Crane count times crane rate sets port stay. Port stay against the contracted berth window sets reputation. Yard density times the inverse of dwell sets yard throughput. Gate turn time sets lorry queues on the public roads. Each link is sourced below, and each becomes a player decision. Liner services book recurring **berthing windows**, a ship that misses its window can lose the call, and terminals agree windows rather than exact times ([UFES thesis](https://core.ac.uk/works/45683095); [Laumanns et al.](https://core.ac.uk/works/52978140); [Golias et al.](https://iaorifors.com/paper/67996); [Univ. of Moratuwa](https://dl.lib.uom.lk/items/01226c98-6853-4678-8bf0-3386766ff573)) **[Fact]**. The World Bank and S&P **CPPI** ranks 403 ports on port hours per call, which is a ready-made end-of-year scoreboard ([S&P](https://press.spglobal.com/2025-09-22-Port-Performance-Varies-Across-the-Globe-Amid-Continuing-Shocks); [Marhaba](https://marhaba.qa/hamad-port-ranks-first-in-the-gulf-11th-globally-in-the-container-port-performance-index-2024/); [Breakbulk News](https://breakbulk.news/container-port-performance-index-2020-2024-global-shifts-and-regional-lessons/)) **[Fact]**. Automation has a sourced downside to model: McKinsey and CENIT find automated terminals often under-deliver on productivity even where operating costs fall ([Lloyd's List](https://lloydslist.maritimeintelligence.informa.com/LL1125824/Ports-are-failing-at-automation-says-McKinsey); [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/cost-operational-challenges-hinder-port-automation); [ECTRI](https://www.ectri.org/new-cenit-report-on-automated-container-terminals/)) **[Fact]**, which suggests a ramp-up penalty **[Inference]**.

If **1 game hour = 60 real seconds at 1x**, that is exactly **30 moves per crane-hour**, the "typical good terminal" figure. So keeping M0's feel and the real rates is the same thing **[Inference]**. ## Godot 4 can carry this if M0's view and tick change first

M0's architecture (pure RefCounted sim plus a separate view) is well placed, because the sim can move off the main thread and be tested headless. Three of its choices will not scale, though. **[Fact]** Godot's docs recommend moving from nodes to the RenderingServer at "thousands" of per-frame instances and to MultiMesh at larger counts. A MultiMesh is one draw primitive with all-or-none culling, so the docs suggest "several MultiMeshes for different areas of the world". The recommended pool pattern is to allocate a maximum `instance_count` and vary `visible_instance_count`. Per-instance colour and custom data are built in, and `set_buffer_interpolated` supports a fixed-tick sim ([MultiMesh tutorial](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/using_multimesh.html); [MultiMesh class](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_multimesh.html); [Servers](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/using_servers.html); [GPU optimization](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/gpu_optimization.html)). A 2026 forum thread notes that shared-mesh MeshInstances now get automatic instancing, which narrows the gap. It still recommends chunked MultiMeshes, but the evidence is anecdotal ([Godot Forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/multimesh-vs-renderingserver-meshinstances-for-an-extremely-high-number-of-instances/143319)). Visibility ranges (HLOD) and import-time mesh LOD both work with MultiMeshInstance3D. Occlusion culling helps mainly on the Mobile renderer ([Visibility ranges](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.html); [Mesh LOD](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/mesh_lod.html); [Occlusion culling](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/occlusion_culling.html)). No measured Godot 4 benchmark of MeshInstance3D against MultiMesh was found, so Port Skylines must build its own benchmark scene **[Gap]**.

On the sim side, **[Fact]** `_physics_process` gives a fixed rate independent of framerate ([Idle and physics processing](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/idle_and_physics_processing.html)). Typed GDScript uses optimised opcodes: about 25-50% faster operations and about 120-150% faster native calls in the author's benchmarks, with JIT/AOT still only "planned" ([Godot blog](https://godotengine.org/article/gdscript-progress-report-typed-instructions); [Static typing](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/gdscript/static_typing.html); [beep.blog](https://www.beep.blog/2024-02-14-gdscript-typing/); [PR #70838](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/70838)). C# was "roughly ~4×" GDScript in naive 4.0 cases, with marshalling costs; this is a secondhand quote ([Godot Forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/state-of-gdscript-vs-c-performance-in-godot-4-0/5875)). **AStarGrid2D is not thread-safe**: use one AStar object per thread. The scene tree is not thread-safe either, while WorkerThreadPool group tasks spread per-element work across threads ([Thread-safe APIs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/thread_safe_apis.html); [WorkerThreadPool](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_workerthreadpool.html); [Using multiple threads](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/using_multiple_threads.html); [AStarGrid2D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_astargrid2d.html)). The profiler comes first ([CPU optimization](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/cpu_optimization.html)). A forum user got about 15 FPS with only 50 physics-body enemies, which suggests per-agent nodes and physics, rather than GDScript, are what kill many-agent sims ([Godot Forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/trying-to-run-atleast-1k-enemies-but-goes-at-15-fps-with-just-50-enemies/125229)). An AStarGrid2D path-correctness bug is referenced in PR #86983 ([itch.io devlog](https://marcosd.itch.io/marcos-mysterious-maze/devlog/697870/checking-godots-source-to-confirm-suspicion-about-astargrid2d)) **[Unverified, search summary]**.

**[Inference]** The escalation order is: fixed tick plus typed GDScript now; then packed-array vehicle state, cached routes and flow fields (lorries travel between a handful of gates, stacks and berths), and a background pathfinding thread with its own AStar copy; then WorkerThreadPool; and only if the profiler proves a hot loop over a few milliseconds, a C++ or Rust GDExtension for that loop ([godot-rust](https://godot-rust.github.io/book/); [Megastructures devlog](https://klaim.itch.io/megastructures/devlog/586698/devlog-2023-09-09)). Grid roads stay the sim truth. If CS-style curves are added later, circular arcs avoid the Bézier "pinching" problem and make intersection maths O(1) ([Sandbox Spirit](https://www.sandboxspirit.com/blog/art-of-roads-in-games); [DiVA thesis](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1675311/FULLTEXT02); [GTPlanet](https://gtplanet.net/forum/threads/cities-skylines-the-sim-city-we-deserve.325276/page-23); [Godot Road Generator](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/theduckcow/godot-road-generator)). A flat reclaimed plane plus a water shader likely beats Terrain3D unless hills matter ([GameFromScratch](https://gamefromscratch.com/terrain3d-for-godot-hits-1-0-release/); [Terrain3D](https://github.com/TokisanGames/Terrain3D); [Asset Store](https://store.godotengine.org/asset/tokisangames/terrain3d/)).

Saves and mods carry a security constraint. **[Fact]** Resources, ConfigFile and str2var can execute embedded code. `FileAccess.get_var` refuses objects by default. PCK mods run with full privileges and Godot has no script sandbox ([GDQuest](https://www.gdquest.com/library/save_game_godot4/); [FileAccess](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_fileaccess.html); [Saving games](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/io/saving_games.html); [Safe Resource Loader](https://github.com/derkork/godot-safe-resource-loader); [Exporting packs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_pcks.html)). Godot Mod Loader (CC0; used by Brotato and Dome Keeper) and GodotSteam's UGC class cover Workshop ([Asset Library](https://www.godotengine.org/asset-library/asset/4107); [GitHub](https://github.com/GodotModding/godot-mod-loader); [GodotSteam UGC](https://godotsteam.com/classes/ugc/); [Workshop tutorial](https://godotsteam.com/tutorials/workshop/); [godotsteam.com](https://godotsteam.com/)). **[Inference]** Saves should be `{version, ...}` dictionaries written with `store_var`, with one `migrate_vN()` step per version. That fits the developer's "one way, fail fast" style: an unknown version is an error, never a silent fallback.

On the engine version, **[Fact]** M0 is on 4.4.1. Since then 4.5 added the Shader Baker, `@abstract`, variadics, reliable deep `duplicate` and screen-reader support. 4.6 added delta patch PCKs, better LOD and D3D12 as the Windows default. 4.7 (mid-June 2026) is current stable, and 4.8 is in dev snapshots ([4.4](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.4/); [4.5](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.5/); [4.6](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.6/); [4.7](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.7/); [Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-06-12-godot-4-7-rc); [wnhub](https://wnhub.io/news/engines/item-51174)). **[Inference]** Upgrade once, in the foundations phase while the codebase is small, then freeze on a patch release until after EA launch. Consoles require third-party middleware. W4 Starter is $800/yr for one platform, and C# on consoles is still beta on Switch and Xbox only ([Godot consoles](https://godotengine.org/consoles/); [Godot docs 4.5 consoles](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.5/tutorials/platform/consoles.html); [W4](https://w4games.com/2023/12/11/w4-games-announces-pricing-model-for-console-ports/); [GameFromScratch W4](https://gamefromscratch.com/w4-games-release-godot-w4-console-pricing/); [W4 Consoles](https://www.w4games.com/w4consoles); [GamingOnLinux RAWRLAB](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/01/godot-engine-gets-a-free-nintendo-switch-port-for-game-devs/page=1/)). Staying on GDScript plus an optional C++ GDExtension keeps that door open. Godot has shipped city builders: Of Life and Land went to 1.0 after about 13 months of EA, and ZIMBY credits Godot 4 threading for its performance. Neither has published a performance postmortem ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/03/of-life-and-land-settlement-builder-releases-april-2); [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/04/peaceful-nature-simulation-city-builder-of-life-and-land-launches-may-16); [ZIMBY devlog](https://cawlgames.itch.io/zimby/devlog/495547/zombie-civilian-ai); [Pity Please](https://pitypleasethegame.duckdns.org/); [Neonroof](https://neonroof.com/news/godot-rust-a-powerful-open-source-stack-for-the-next-generation-of-rts-games)) **[Fact]**.

### M1: Foundations that every later phase depends on

**Goal.** Remove the structural shortcuts before content piles on top of them. **Features.** None that the player sees, apart from smoother motion and save/load. **Technical work.** Replace `_process` variable-delta stepping with a fixed-tick accumulator (for example 10-20 sim ticks per game-hour, N ticks per frame for each speed, capped per frame) and interpolate the view between ticks. Type all GDScript. Move ship, crane, lorry and equipment definitions into data files. Add `to_dict`/`from_dict` per sim class and a versioned `store_var` save that rejects unknown versions. Upgrade Godot from 4.4.1 to the chosen 4.6/4.7 patch release. Replace one-MeshInstance3D-per-tile with chunked MultiMeshes (for example 32×32-tile chunks per mesh type). Add a benchmark scene and a headless soak test. **Exit criteria (testable).** The same seed and input log give a byte-identical state hash after 10,000 ticks at 1x and at 3x. Save → load → save gives identical bytes. The existing headless tests and CI stay green on the new engine version. A 256×256 synthetic map renders with draw calls proportional to chunks, not tiles, and the frame time is recorded as the baseline.

### M2: A real terminal loop worth planning

**Goal.** Turn "ship drops 12 boxes" into the berth → crane → yard → gate chain from the tuning table. **Features.** Berths with length and depth, and ships of two classes (Feeder, Panamax) that need a matching berth. STS cranes as placed entities with outreach and a moves/hour rate. Boxes move crane → horizontal transport → stack, so the instant teleport goes. Import and export flows with dwell. Yard blocks with equipment type (reach stacker, RTG) and stack capacity. A gate building with lanes and a visible queue. Lorries that queue rather than passing through each other. Upkeep and running costs. **Technical work.** Cell- or slot-reservation traffic on the grid with simple intersection reservation, which is deterministic and fits M1's tick. A route cache keyed on (from, to) and invalidated on road edits. Start KPIs: berth productivity, crane rate, gate turn time and yard utilisation. **Exit criteria.** Two lorries never occupy the same cell (a test asserts this every tick). A Panamax with 120 moves and 3 cranes at 25/h finishes in moves/(cranes×rate×0.9) ±5% of game time. Yard throughput tracks capacity/dwell within ±10% in a soak test. A blind playtester can explain why a queue formed.

### M3: Contracts, a legible economy and milestone unlocks

**Goal.** Give the player goals and trade-offs, copying CS2's milestone gating and transparent money rather than its hidden economy. **Features.** Shipping lines offering weekly berth-window contracts with promised productivity, and reputation that drops on misses. Cascading delays for late ships. Storage fees and free days. Channel depth with dredging as capex plus opex. Crane orders with lead time. A milestone ladder (TEU per year) unlocking ship classes, equipment and panels. A ledger showing every income and cost line. A bankruptcy or loan rule. **Technical work.** An event and schedule system in the tick. The save format bumps to v2 with a migration. Content data gains costs and unlocks. **Exit criteria.** Scripted scenarios prove that over-selling windows causes misses, that raising fees lowers average dwell, and that the ledger sums to the cash delta exactly every game day (asserted). A 2-hour playtest reaches the third milestone without a dead end.

### M4: A vertical slice good enough to sell from

**Goal.** Produce screenshots, a trailer and a store page that represent the game honestly. **Features.** A first art-direction pass on the core set (quay, STS, RTG, boxes, lorries, water), a 15-minute guided start, and a basic UI theme. **Technical work.** Import-time LOD on real meshes, the Shader Baker for stutter, and a first performance budget per entity type. **Business work.** The name is resolved and cleared before this phase starts. Create the Steam page as "Coming soon" with a trailer, and set up a devlog or mailing list. **Exit criteria.** The page is live, the trailer is under 90 s, the game holds 60 fps on the target spec in the slice map, and five outside playtesters complete the opening without help.

### M5: Scale, rail and the "outside world"

**Goal.** Reach the entity counts a mature port needs, and add the second land mode against truck spam. **Features.** On-dock rail sidings with rail cranes and scheduled trains. A hinterland demand model (import destinations and export origins off-map). Neopanamax class, deeper dredging and wider-outreach cranes. Reefer plugs and electricity demand charges. Wind events. Larger maps or extra purchasable tiles with upkeep. **Technical work.** Vehicle state as parallel packed arrays, per-type vehicle MultiMesh with the buffer written once per frame (`set_buffer_interpolated`), flow fields or Dijkstra maps from gates, pathfinding on a background thread with its own AStar instance, WorkerThreadPool for kinematics, and HLOD for distant yards. Profile first. Consider GDExtension only for a loop that measures above budget. **Exit criteria.** The endgame benchmark save (for example 2,000 lorries, 20,000 stacked boxes, 6 berths, 2 trains, all numbers to be set by the developer) holds the agreed frame-time budget on minimum spec. Sim ticks stay deterministic with threading on (hash test). Shifting 30% of volume to rail measurably cuts the gate queue.

### M6: Demo and Steam Next Fest

**Goal.** Turn wishlists into evidence. **Features.** A time- or milestone-limited demo with saves disabled or separate, and in-game feedback and crash reporting. **Technical work.** Export pipeline for Windows and Linux (plus Steam Deck checks if chosen), GodotSteam integration, and a build that holds up for an hour-long stream. **Exit criteria.** Next Fest is completed, the demo crash rate is below the agreed threshold, the median session length is recorded, and the top five player complaints are logged with fixes scheduled for M7.

### M7: Early Access readiness

**Goal.** A narrow, stable, fun EA build, not a wide one. **Features.** All four ship classes (ULCV gated late), straddle and RMG equipment tiers, sandbox plus 3-5 scenarios, settings (keybinds, UI scale, colour-blind palette for line colours), the save migration policy published, data-file moddability (if chosen), achievements and Cloud. **Technical work.** A save-compatibility test that loads every previous save fixture in CI, plus a crash reporter and a performance regression test in CI. **Exit criteria.** 10 hours of soak with no crash or desync, every save fixture loads, the performance budget is met, the store page lists only shipped features (Valve says not to promise future features), and the EA price is no higher than elsewhere ([Steamworks EA docs](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/earlyaccess) **[Unverified: search summary]**).

### Early Access to 1.0 and beyond

**[Estimate]** Plan 4-8 content updates over 2-4 years solo, each taking one system from abstract to simulated. Likely candidates, in order of evidence strength: the city behind the port (the "outside world" link players found missing in CS2), transhipment and feeder relays, automation tiers with the ramp-up penalty, customs and inspection, barge, shore-power regulation, strikes and congestion events, a campaign, and Workshop. Community proposals of "at least every 3 months" are not a Valve rule ([Steam EA guidelines group](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/EarlyAccessGuidelines/discussions/0/412448158154749096); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/early-access-rules/)), but slow updates from a tiny team draw criticism ([Metacritic W&R](https://www.metacritic.com/game/workers-and-resources-soviet-republic/)) **[Fact]**. **1.0 exit criteria:** feature-complete against the public roadmap, localisation, performance on minimum spec with the largest map, and a price rise (Farthest Frontier went from $29.99 to $34.99) ([Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/article/2025/9/26/news/147749-farthest-frontier-leaving-early-access-after-3-years-gets-october-release-date/)). **Post-1.0:** paid expansions priced against Bridges & Ports' "20 Euro, really?" reaction, script mods via Mod Loader with a trust warning, and consoles through W4 or a porting house only once controller UI and performance are proven **[Inference]**.

**What these answers change** **[Inference]**:
- **Release phases.** A free hobby release going straight to 1.0 removes the wishlist gates, the Next Fest timing and the Early Access sections. M6 becomes public playtest builds, for example on itch.io or a free Steam page (Steam still charges its app fee). M7 becomes 1.0 readiness. There is no competitor race to win, so phases can run until their exit criteria pass rather than to a date.
- **Name.** The trademark risk does not go away because the game is free. Using SKYLINES on a published game can still draw an objection, so a name change is still possible later. Not legal advice.
- **Scope against time.** The chosen scope is far larger than the single terminal that the 24–30 month estimate assumed. It adds a whole port, three transport modes, CS2-style routing, semi-realistic art, three event types and automation, all built solo with no Early Access feedback. An honest solo estimate becomes about 5–8 years full-time to 1.0. To keep the game playable throughout, build in this order:
  1. One terminal, road only, working end to end.
  2. Rail.
  3. A second terminal.
  4. Barge and feeder transhipment.
  5. Events.
  6. Automation.
- **Traffic.** CS2-style cost routing decides which route a vehicle takes. It does not stop vehicles occupying the same space, so cell or slot reservation from M2 is still needed underneath it. Cost routing over a lane graph moves into M5.
- **Two clocks.** A day/night calendar decoupled from vehicle speed makes dwell, free days and contracts tunable without changing how fast things move. Add it in M1 alongside the fixed tick.
- **Saves.** "Migrate forever" makes M1's versioned save format and the CI save-fixture test mandatory from the first save.

## Open questions for the developer

Each answer changes phase content, ordering or duration. Answers given so far are in **Decisions so far** above.

1. **Final name.** "Skylines" carries high, not-legally-assessed risk. It matters because the Steam page, domain, trailer and wishlists all attach to the name, and renaming after M4 discards that work. Options: rename now (recommended), get an attorney's clearance opinion first, or keep the name and accept the risk of a forced rebrand.
2. **Team size and roles.** This sets every duration above, roughly 2x between solo and a small team. Options: solo plus bought assets; solo plus contract artist; a 3-6 person team.
3. **Budget and runway, and whether this is full-time.** Genre EA lasts 3-5 years, so runway decides whether EA has to happen early for income. Options: self-funded runway of N months; publisher advance; EA revenue only; hobby pace.
4. **Commercial goal.** It decides how much marketing, demo and Next Fest work to schedule. Options: hobby or portfolio; sustainable income (around Rise of Industry scale); breakout attempt with a publisher.
5. **PC operating systems.** Each one adds export, QA and support work. Options: Windows only; Windows plus Linux (cheap in Godot, and genre peers do it); plus macOS.
6. **Steam Deck target.** It sets UI scale, controller support, renderer and performance budget. Options: ignore; "Playable" best-effort; target "Verified" from M6.
7. **Consoles.** This affects language choice, UI design and middleware cost ($800+/yr W4). Options: never; post-1.0 via W4 self-port; via a porting house or publisher.
8. **Art direction.** It is the largest variance in M4 and M7 effort and decides positioning against Portscape's colourful miniature look. Options: clean low-poly diorama; semi-realistic industrial; stylised night control-room; bought asset kits.
9. **Realism versus cozy.** This is the core differentiator. Options: realistic planner (recommended by the gap analysis); cozy-realistic hybrid; cozy (direct collision with Portscape).
10. **Player role.** It decides whether equipment is ever player-driven. Options: pure planner with policies; planner with optional manual dispatch (a Port Control overlap); some direct crane control (a Docked overlap).
11. **Grid versus free-form roads and quays.** This decides pathfinding, rendering and road-tool effort. Options: grid only; grid sim with arc-rounded visual corners; full spline roads (adds months, and arcs are recommended over Béziers).
12. **Scope of the simulated world.** This is the biggest scope lever. Options: one terminal; a whole port with several terminals, channel and pilots; port plus a growing city or hinterland (CS-like). Recommended: one terminal at EA and the city as an EA update.
13. **Map size, tile scale and expansion model.** It sets performance targets and ULCV feasibility, since a 500 m berth at 25 m per tile needs 20 tiles and M0's map is 64×48. Options: fixed larger map; CS-style purchasable tiles with upkeep; procedural coastlines.
14. **Time scale.** It decides how dwell, contracts and lead times feel. Options: a single clock (1 game-hour = 60 s at 1x, with compressed calendar values as proposed); separate vehicle and calendar clocks as in CS; a turn or day planning layer.
15. **Game modes at EA.** It changes M7 content. Options: sandbox only; sandbox plus scenarios; a campaign (more writing and tuning).
16. **Failure and difficulty.** It shapes economy tuning and the tutorial. Options: bankruptcy game-over; loans and bailouts; no-fail creative mode; selectable difficulty.
17. **Economy depth.** It changes M3 scope. Options: simple revenue per box plus upkeep; contracts, fees and reputation (recommended); a full market with competing terminals and line negotiation.
18. **Traffic model depth.** It sets M2 and M5 effort and the risk of gridlock bugs. Options: cell reservation on a grid (recommended first); lane-level with turn costs and signals; CS2-style cost routing with parking.
19. **Which land and sea modes ship at EA.** Each mode is months of work. Options: road only; road plus on-dock rail (recommended, as it answers the truck-spam lesson); plus barge or feeder transhipment.
20. **Automation and policies versus micromanagement.** It sets UI and AI work. Options: global policies; per-block rules; automated-terminal tech tier with ramp-up penalty.
21. **Disasters and events.** These add drama but risk frustration. Options: none at EA; wind only; wind plus strikes, congestion and regulations (shore power), each toggleable.
22. **Modding at launch.** CS2's late delivery hurt trust. Options: none promised; data-file mods at EA (cheap if M1 goes data-driven); Workshop at EA; script mods via Mod Loader post-1.0.
23. **Early Access or straight to 1.0.** It changes the whole back half. Options: EA (the genre norm); a direct 1.0 like Banished (longer silent build); EA with a short, dated plan (Manor Lords overran its one).
24. **Price, monetisation and DLC.** It drives revenue expectations and EA promises. Options: $19.99-24.99 EA rising at 1.0 (recommended range); premium plus paid expansions; free updates only. Avoid cosmetic-only paid packs priced like expansions.
25. **Publisher or self-publish.** A publisher trades revenue share for marketing reach, QA and possibly an advance, and moves the pitch to M4. Options: self-publish; a genre specialist (for example Hooded Horse); a porting or console-only partner later.
26. **Godot version policy.** It affects stutter (Shader Baker), LOD and patch size (delta PCKs). Options: upgrade to 4.6/4.7 in M1 and pin (recommended); stay on 4.4.x; track every minor release.
27. **Language and native code.** It affects performance headroom and the console path. Options: GDScript only; GDScript plus a C++ or Rust GDExtension for hot loops when profiled (recommended); C# (needs the .NET build and has beta console support).
28. **Renderer and minimum spec.** It sets the performance budget and Deck and low-end reach. Options: Forward+ only; Forward+ plus Mobile/Compatibility fallback; a published minimum GPU.
29. **Endgame performance budget.** M5's exit criteria need concrete numbers. Options: the developer sets target counts (lorries, boxes, berths, trains) and frame time on minimum spec.
30. **Save compatibility policy in EA.** It decides how much migration code each update carries. Options: every save migrates forever; breaking saves allowed at major updates with notice; a beta branch for breaking changes.
31. **Real-world brands and places.** Real shipping-line names, liveries and port maps add authenticity but also legal risk. Options: fictional lines and ports (recommended); real geography with fictional companies; licensed brands.
32. **Repository visibility and licensing.** The repo is on GitHub. Public code invites contributions but also clones of the concept before launch. Options: keep private until EA; open source the engine and keep assets proprietary; fully open.
33. **Localisation and accessibility at EA.** It adds M7 work and affects reach. Options: English only at EA; top 5 Steam languages at 1.0; colour-blind palettes and screen-reader support (Godot 4.5 added the latter) from M7.
34. **Public roadmap and update cadence.** Promises create EA obligations. Options: rolling "next update" only; a quarterly public roadmap; a dated roadmap to 1.0.
35. **Roads first, then areas and assets.** The developer proposed replacing square-by-square building with roads drawn first, areas laid out along them, and assets inside each area (for example a handover lane where an AGV waits), with full crane animations for each move. **Decided:** it is M5, with its steps and answers in [docs/phases/m05-roads-and-areas.md](phases/m05-roads-and-areas.md). It replaces decision 11's square-by-square building with roads on a 4 m grid.

## Conclusion

The research changes the main question from "can a port game work?" to "which port game, and how fast?". Docked's sales prove the theme sells. Portscape, Port Control and Harbor Master prove other developers see the same gap, and each has taken a different corner of it. What none of them claims is the planner's view, in which real numbers such as 25-35 moves per crane-hour, density divided by dwell, and 22 m/s wind limits become the puzzle itself. That is where Port Skylines should stand. M0 is also unusually well calibrated already: its one-box-per-2-seconds rate equals a realistic 30 moves per crane-hour at a one-minute game hour, so realism and feel do not conflict.

The bigger lesson from Cities: Skylines II is about order, not features. A fixed tick, versioned saves, data-driven content and a benchmark save cost a solo developer about two months now, and they are what make every later phase testable and every EA update safe. The two decisions with the shortest deadline are the name, before the Steam page exists, and the scope line (one terminal versus port plus city). Both should be made before M2 starts.
