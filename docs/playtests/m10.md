# M10 playtest: roads like Cities: Skylines

**Date:** 10 Nov 2026. **Build:** branch `claude/new-session-bj34e4`. **How:**
scripted, not a public build. Checked through the tests and the soak, the
showcase port (debug menu → *Showcase port*), and the real scene
(`main.tscn`) under `xvfb-run` at 1280×720, by screenshot. The public
playtest build still needs a person to play it; the list at the end is
what they should try.

## What was checked

| Area | How |
|---|---|
| Roads at any angle | Roads meet, cross and split at any angle down to the junction rules' least angle; a road is refused where a road at a junction it touches would be shorter than its ends are cut back (`test_road_meetings`, `test_roads`). |
| Curves | A road curves through a control point, no tighter than its type's `min_radius_cells`; pieces split at crossings keep their part of the curve (`test_road_curves`, `test_curved_roads`). |
| Snapping | Onto roads and the highway's anchor, onto guide lines from dead ends and beside straight roads, 15° steps and whole cells; Grid snap keeps straight or 45° on grid points (`test_road_snapping`). |
| Lanes and traffic | Vehicles follow lanes along curves, keep a gap, change lane where marked, and obey lane arrows and roundabout give-way (`test_lane_network`, `test_lane_changes`, `test_lane_arrows`, `test_roundabouts`, `test_traffic`). |
| Move It | A road's node or middle moves with its areas, or is refused with a reason, leaving the port as it was (`test_move_it`). |
| Areas on curves | Yards and gates are laid along any curve and lose cells on a tight inside bend (`test_area_shape`, `test_curved_yard`). |
| Rail | Track through cells' middles, curved no tighter than 48 m; trains follow it within 0.01 cells and 2° (61° off on the old cell path; verified to fail that way, `test_rail`). Save v45–v46 migrate track and trains. |
| Ship lanes | Lanes at any angle or curved (200, 300 and 80 m for narrow, wide and river); ships sail along the curve and book a narrow lane once (`test_ship_lanes`). Save v47 gives lanes a control point. |
| Berths | A berth turns, in 15° steps, to the angle that fills least, or as Turn sets it; along the grid it is the berth it always was. On the estuary's slanting bank a west-facing berth fills 634 cells and the turned one 58 (`test_berths`, `test_coast`). |
| Showcase | Builds with nothing refused and works its ships, with a curved yard road (four blocks on its bend, all at work) to a roundabout, the track's first corner on a curve and the river channel curving down the river (`test_showcase`; screenshots). |
| Soak | Half a game day of the showcase port under every soak invariant (`test_soak`). |
| Performance | The endgame save, M10's gate: 12.2–14.3 ms a tick over two runs against 19.5 (15.65 at step 11) (`profile_sim.gd -- 200`). The sim bench's worst tick 9–13 ms, average about 2.5 ms. |

## Found and fixed

1. **A gate got no exit at a lane change's end**, as the nearest lane went to the change, not the road; road lanes win ties now. Lorries also held a lane change on short roads.
2. **The soak slowed from 186 s to 326 s** once roads met at any angle: stub roads shorter than their junction cut lanes backwards into curves thousands of cells long. Such roads are refused now, and conflicts are found only between neighbouring segments (216 s).
3. **A one-way road into a junction crashed its connections**: it has no lane out.
4. **Half of each road's asphalt was culled** by the winding of its quads.
5. **Track counted cells its line ran through only some of the time**: its quads' middles fell on cell edges. Its ground is now the cells its line crosses.
6. **Trains on a curve swung up to 64° off the track**, stepping cell to cell; they run on points along it now.
7. **Quays read through the apron's shape cost the worst tick 90–120 ms**: every point built a curve. Curves are built once and pockets kept.
8. **An angled berth could pass its check and fail to build**: its road was looked for by a cell it need not cover, and its fill missed water under its road.

## Open

1. **An apron's stored `start`, `end` and `out` round a turned apron to the grid.** Nothing places anything by them since step 21, but tests still do; removing them needs those tests rewritten and a save step.
2. **Yard pavement is drawn on one side of a road only**: blocks on the other side show their machines on bare ground. It predates the curves (the grid yard lanes show it too).
3. **The showcase's berths still face west**, as its grid connectors expect; it has no berth at its own angle.
4. **Radii are first guesses** in the data: rail 48 m, ship lanes 200, 300 and 80 m, berth turns 15°.
5. **Grid snap draws no guide lines**, and a road's start isn't marked where it joins a road.
6. **The tools haven't been driven by hand**: drawing curved roads, track and lanes, Move It and turning berths were checked by tests and scripted screenshots only.

## For the public playtest

- Lay a curving spine road and yards along its bends; check blocks on the inside of a tight bend come out shorter.
- Build a roundabout on a busy junction and watch whether it moves traffic better than signals.
- Draw curved track to an RMG block and watch a train take the bend.
- Draw a river channel along the river's bends with the curved Shape.
- Place berths on the estuary's slanting banks; try Turn and Auto, and compare the fill cost.
- Use Move It to reshape a road with yards along it.
