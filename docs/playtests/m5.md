# M5 playtest: one full chain on roads and areas

**Date:** 19 Oct 2026. **Build:** `claude/roads-and-areas` at M5 step 24.
**How:** the real scene (`main.tscn`) under `xvfb-run` with the Compatibility
renderer, played by the same commands the toolbar and Inspect send, on the
base rules from the $20,000 start money (seed 1). Screenshots were checked by
eye at each step; the numbers below come from the sim at those moments.

## The chain

| Step | What happened |
|---|---|
| Roads | A 1+1 terminal road from the highway to the coast, meeting a coast road that runs 40 m in from the water and on past the junction both ways. $1,300. |
| Areas | A 240 m quay apron on the coast road's sea side, a gate by the highway, and an import and an export block on the coast road's land side. Inspect on the gate joined it to the apron's terminal. |
| Equipment | One STS crane (up the next day) and four tractors. The whole chain cost $7,900; upkeep $680 a day. |
| Contracts | Three lines' weekly feeder services accepted 32 game hours in. |
| First ship | Day 1 15:00. Worked in 6.0 h for 40 moves, on time; every call after it the same. |
| Money | Moves earned $5,900 on day 1 and $10,000 on day 2, against $990 upkeep. |
| RTGs | Milestone 2 (200 TEU) on day 3.2. Switching the import block to RTGs puts an RTG over its stacks. |
| One box | STS → tractor → RTG block slot → stacked by the RTG → lifted onto a lorry → gate → off site, about 150 game minutes in all. The follow camera tracks a box from the crane into the yard. |

The exit criteria hold: one full chain plays with an RTG yard block and the
main motions animated, the follow camera tracks a box, the replay hash test
passes at 1, 3 and 6 ticks per frame, and tile saves are refused with a
clear message beside a v11 fixture.

## Found and fixed during the playtest

1. **The guide's first layout made every call late.** A yard block on one
   straight road has nowhere to turn but the highway, 2 km away: vehicles
   turn round only at dead ends. Each export took 0.9 h to fetch (a
   1,081-cell route), so a feeder took 17.7 h, 6 to 17 h past its window.
   The guide now has the coast road run on past the junction both ways, with
   the yard behind the apron: the fetch is 85 cells and the call 6.0 h.
   `test_guide` fails a first feeder worked outside its window.
2. **Switching a block to RTGs changed nothing.** It kept its reach stackers
   and their stack height. Machines of the old gear now go as they finish
   their box, the new gear's arrive, and stacks take its height. Tested in
   `test_rules`.

## Open, for later phases

*Status on 24 Oct 2026:* 1, 2, 3 and 4 are fixed: a loading crane keeps up
to four tractors fetching; export lorries come only for booked calls, up to
48 h ahead; lorries use the gate nearest them and a two-way gate puts one on
each side of the road; an apron stretches to the water from a road 44–68 m
in. 5, 6 and 7 are M6 steps. 5 is worse than written: the second playtest
below measured every vehicle at 80 m a game minute, below both data speeds.

1. **One tractor per crane serialises loading.** A crane waits for each
   export's whole round trip: 6.0 h for 40 moves where the crane's own work
   is 1.8 h. A queue of tractors per crane would close most of that gap (M6).
2. **Exports arrive whatever ships need.** Export lorries come at a fixed
   rate until the export block is full: 300 boxes before the first ship, and
   their storage pays $5,000 a day, half the port's income. M6's hinterland
   demand should drive them.
3. **Gates work one way.** A gate is laid along one side of a road, so an
   outbound lorry turns round at a dead end to pass it and again to leave:
   65 minutes of gate turn. A gate across both directions, or in and out
   gates, would fix it.
4. **A quay apron needs its road exactly 40 m from the water,** and the road
   tool doesn't show where that is. Snap or mark the coast line when
   drawing near the sea.
5. **Speeds are the tile game's:** lorries at 125 m and tractors at 150 m a
   game minute (7.5 and 9 km/h), so a 2 km trip takes 15 minutes. Tune them
   with M6's scale work.
6. **An STS crane can't be removed on its own;** only bulldozing its apron
   takes it down.
7. **Ships and STS cranes are still drawn at the tile view's scale,** so a
   ship's deck boxes read larger than the real-size boxes in the yard.

# Second playtest: after the junction, crane, export and gate fixes

**Date:** 25 Oct 2026. **Build:** `main` at the merge of PR #9.
**How:** `tools/playtest.gd`, which builds the guide's layout by the
commands the toolbar sends, on the base rules from the start money (seed 1),
and plays 7 game days accepting offers up to the guide's 3 services. The
coast road is drawn 52 m in from the water, and the terminal road is
dragged to a point 2 cells beside it, so the apron's stretch and the road
tool's snapping are played as a mouse would. It ran with a one-way gate
(on the inbound lane's side, as the guide asks) and a two-way gate, each
80 m in from the highway and again by the coast T. Screenshots of
the real scene under `xvfb-run` were checked by eye at the apron, the gate
and the T junction with traffic passing.

## Results

| | M5 playtest | One-way gate | Two-way gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feeder call, 40 moves | 6.0 h | 2.8 h | 2.8 h |
| Gate turn, gate by the highway | 65 min | 63 min | 60 min |
| Gate turn, gate by the coast T | — | 63 min | 20 min |
| Storage income | half of all income | $0 | $0 |
| Exports in the yard before calls | 300 | 32, then 16 for next week's calls | same |
| Lorries that gave up, worst gate queue | — | 0, 0 | 0, 0 |
| Money after 7 days, from $20,000 | — | $22,959 | $21,924 |

- **Snapping:** the terminal road dragged to (90, 301) ended at (88, 300) on
  the coast road and made a 3-way junction.
- **Apron:** 13 rows deep from a road 52 m in, reaching the water.
- **Calls** take 2.8 h against 1.8 h of crane work: a loading crane keeps
  its tractors busy now.

## Found

1. **Every vehicle moves at 80 m a game minute, whatever the data says.**
   Measured: lorries moving to a block cover 20.0 cells a game minute; the
   data's 125 m is 31.2. `LaneTraffic.advance` enters at most one new cell a
   tick (its target is capped at `i + 1.99`), and a game minute is 20 ticks,
   so the cap is 20 cells, 4.8 km/h. Tractors (150 m) are capped the same,
   so lorries and tractors run at one speed. The M6 step (now corrected) said
   the cap was about 160 m; it is half that.
2. **Gate turns are driving.** Of 8,000 lorry minutes between gate in and
   gate out, 92% were spent driving to a block or back to a gate, none
   stopped in traffic, 3% at the booth and 2% being handled. Routes between
   gate and blocks are 480–690 cells each way, against 489 in a straight
   line. So a turn is the round trip at the capped speed; finding 1 is most
   of it.
3. **A two-way gate pays only where turning round is far away.** A lorry
   past a one-way gate drives the whole loop to turn round, 1,187 cells
   wherever the gate stands. Beside the highway, the two-way gate's other
   side is reached by turning at the coast T anyway: 1,132 cells, 60 minutes
   against 63. By the coast T it cuts the trip to 332 cells: 20 minutes
   against 63. (A first run put the one-way gate on the outbound side and
   compared gates at different places; its numbers are not used here.)
4. **Income comes in two days a week.** The three services accepted on day 1
   all called on days 1 and 2; days 3 to 6 earn nothing and cost about
   $1,000 a day in upkeep. The week still nets about $11,000. Offers could
   spread their call days, or the guide could ask for more services.
5. **Storage no longer pays.** With exports only for booked calls and
   imports collected inside the free days, storage income is $0, where it
   was half the M5 port's income. The port is still profitable on moves.
